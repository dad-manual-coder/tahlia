Movements

The currents that shaped history

Every revolution in art began with a refusal to accept what came before. Filter by movement to explore the ideas that shifted everything.

Renaissance

A rebirth of classical ideals. Harmony, proportion, and the dignity of the human form — Da Vinci, Michelangelo, Raphael.

14th – 17th Century

Baroque

Theatricality, grandeur, chiaroscuro. Caravaggio's violence of light; Rembrandt's tender shadows.

17th – 18th Century

Impressionism

Not what the eye sees — but what light does to it. Monet, Renoir, and Degas broke from the rigid academy.

19th Century

Surrealism

Where the unconscious becomes the canvas. Dalí, Magritte, and Ernst painted the iron logic of dreams.

1920s – 1950s

Modernism

Picasso shattered perspective. Matisse dissolved it in colour. Art became an argument about what art could be.

Early 20th Century

Abstract Expressionism

Pollock's drips, Rothko's fields of saturated grief. Pure emotion with no need for recognisable form.

1940s – 1960s

Contemporary

No rules — only questions. Identity, politics, and technology as medium. The conversation is louder than ever.

1970s – Present

Post-Impressionism

Van Gogh, Gauguin, Seurat — each took Impressionism's loosened hand and drove it somewhere deeply personal.

Late 19th Century

History

A thread through time

From cave walls to digital canvases — humanity has always needed to mark its moment.

40,000 BCE

Cave Art & Prehistoric Expression

The first artists pressed ochre-stained hands against limestone at Chauvet and Altamira — not decoration, but testimony: we were here, we saw, we felt.

1300s – 1600s

The Renaissance

Italy rediscovered the classical world and married it to Christian devotion. Perspective was invented. The human body became sacred subject matter. Genius became a category.

1600s – 1700s

Baroque & the Age of Drama

Caravaggio painted saints as peasants, lit from within by a terrifying grace. Art became theatre — shadow was as important as light.

1860s – 1900s

Impressionism & Its Aftermath

Rejected by the official Salon, Monet and his circle painted out of doors. They captured light as sensation rather than fact. The art world was never the same.

1900s – 1940s

Modernism: The Great Rupture

Cubism, Futurism, Dada, Surrealism — each declared that old forms were not just outdated but complicit. Art became an argument about itself.

1940s – 1970s

Abstract Expressionism & Pop

New York inherited Paris's mantle. Pollock dripped, Warhol screened, Rothko glowed. The gallery became a place of almost religious experience.

1970s – Now

Contemporary: Everything Is the Canvas

Identity, politics, technology, trauma — nothing is off-limits. Street art claims public space. Digital art challenges ownership. The conversation is global.
